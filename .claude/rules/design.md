# Visual design requirements

- Do not use emoji as icons, section markers, illustrations, badges, or button decorations. Use a coherent SVG icon family only when icons improve comprehension.
- Develop the visual identity from Marc's real work and personality. Use deliberate typography, spacing, composition, and project imagery rather than a preset landing-page structure.
- Avoid default purple/blue gradients, glowing blobs, glass panels, excessive pills, identical rounded cards in every section, and decorative dashboard widgets unless a specific approved direction justifies them.
- Choose expressive but readable typography, a restrained palette, and consistent tokens for spacing, colors, type, radii, and motion. A monochrome or colorful direction is acceptable when intentional.
- Prioritize selected projects, clear descriptions, and strong imagery. Use layout variety that supports content; do not add pricing, testimonials, counters, or FAQs to fill space.
- Write specific, natural copy. Avoid generic marketing claims and fabricated statistics. Mark missing facts as content questions rather than publishing plausible inventions.
- Keep one clear primary action for each page or major flow. Buttons, navigation, links, filters, and menus must behave as their labels promise.
- Design mobile layouts deliberately. Check at approximately 375px, 768px, and 1440px, plus narrow widths and browser zoom. Prevent horizontal overflow and clipped content.
- Use semantic landmarks, accessible names, visible keyboard focus, logical heading order, adequate contrast, and meaningful alternative text. Decorative SVGs should be hidden from assistive technology.
- Motion should explain interaction or support the visual direction. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; avoid scroll hijacking, compulsory cursor effects, and endless decorative animation.
- Optimize real images, reserve image dimensions, and keep essential content readable before animation or client-side JavaScript finishes.
- Review the final page visually and interactively. A successful build alone does not establish design quality.
